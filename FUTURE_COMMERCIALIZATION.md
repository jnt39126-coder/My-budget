# Possible future commercial version

Saved: August 30, 2026

This document preserves the business idea without changing the current goal: first make My Budget a dependable personal finance app and use it long enough to prove that it helps manage real finances.

## Current decision

- Do not raise capital or build public-user machinery yet.
- Finish the production-safe personal version, use it with real accounts for 30-60 days, and correct anything that does not work naturally in everyday use.
- Consider a private beta only after the personal version is reliable and genuinely useful.

## Potential product position

My Budget could eventually become a privacy-first, ad-free financial command center that combines budgeting, safe-to-spend guidance, debt and credit utilization, investments, retirement, savings goals, and manually entered obligations in one understandable plan.

It should not initially try to match every feature in YNAB, Monarch, or Simplifi. A clearer differentiator would be a calm, straightforward answer to:

> What can I safely spend, what is already committed, and am I improving my overall financial position?

## Evidence required before treating it as a business

1. The personal version works reliably with real accounts for at least 30-60 days.
2. Budget, bill, goal, and manual data synchronize across devices and can be restored.
3. Plaid connection failures are visible and repairable.
4. A private beta of roughly 25-50 people connects accounts and continues returning.
5. At least some beta users voluntarily pay or clearly demonstrate willingness to pay.
6. Per-user Plaid, hosting, support, and payment costs leave a sustainable margin.
7. The product has a credible security, privacy, deletion, and incident-response plan.

## Funding view

- Raising institutional capital now would be difficult and unnecessary because there is no outside-user traction yet.
- A small $25,000-$100,000 angel or friends-and-family round could become realistic after a successful beta and early paid usage.
- A lean, founder-built public launch was estimated at roughly $15,000-$50,000, or approximately $25,000-$75,000 including operating and marketing runway.
- A professionally outsourced commercial build could cost $100,000-$300,000 or more.
- Bootstrapping and early revenue are preferable initially because they preserve ownership and do not impose venture-growth expectations.
- Debt, including an SBA microloan, should be treated cautiously because repayment continues even if the product does not succeed.

## Commercial requirements not needed for the personal version

- Multi-user authentication and strict tenant isolation
- Subscription billing and App Store entitlement verification
- In-app account deletion, customer data export, and support operations
- Formal privacy policy, terms, company entity, regulatory review, and App Store disclosures
- Security audits, monitoring, incident response, and penetration testing
- Customer acquisition, onboarding analytics, and retention measurement

## Possible validation path

1. Finish and personally use the production version.
2. Keep notes on which decisions it improves and where it creates friction.
3. Interview 15-25 potential users before expanding the feature set.
4. Create a narrow landing-page promise and invite-only beta.
5. Measure account-link completion, continued usage, willingness to pay, Plaid cost, support burden, and churn.
6. Only then decide between a small profitable subscription business and a venture-scale company.

## Business hypothesis

A subscription near $7.99-$9.99 per month, with an annual discount, may fit the current budgeting-app market if the product earns trust and provides ongoing value. Profitability would depend more on retention, Plaid cost per connected institution/product, and customer-acquisition cost than on hosting. A focused, lean business might become sustainable with approximately 1,500-3,000 committed paying subscribers, but this is a hypothesis to validate rather than a forecast.
